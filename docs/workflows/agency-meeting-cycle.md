# Workflow — agency meeting cycle

**Journey:** from deciding what to ask, through the briefing package, to minutes and the
commitments that follow. **Documented, not automated.**

This workflow contains the product's clearest human boundary: **the meeting itself is
not a step.** A person represents the sponsor, holds a position, and is accountable for
what they say.

## Before

### 1. Establish the work context

**Skill:** `build-work-context`

### 2. Meeting strategy and type

**Skill:** `select-meeting-type-and-strategy` *(planned)*
Which meeting, when, and what a favourable outcome would actually be.

**Common failure:** a meeting requested to show progress rather than to resolve a
question, which produces advice nobody needed and a commitment nobody wanted.

### 3. Assemble the briefing package

**Skill:** `prepare-briefing-package-content`
Background, the data, and the questions — each question phrased so that any answer
changes what the programme does.

**Carries forward:** the questions, in their final wording. Everything downstream is
scored against them.

### 4. Author the applicant's position

**Skill:** `author-applicants-position` *(planned)*
For each question, the sponsor's position and the evidence it rests on. Including where
the evidence is weaker than the position would like.

### 5. Prepare for the questions behind the questions

**Skill:** `prepare-position-defence-evidence` *(planned, refuse-boundary)*
Assembles anticipated challenges and the evidence for each. **Refuses to defend the
position** — that happens in a room.

---

## 🔴 Gate — package approval and submission

**Sponsor governance approves; a human submits. Submission is an outward action this
product never takes.**

---

## The meeting

**Not a step.** A qualified human represents the sponsor. The library's contribution
ended when the package was submitted and resumes when there are minutes.

---

## After

### 6. Capture minutes and commitments

**Skill:** `capture-meeting-minutes-and-commitments` *(planned)*
What was said, what was agreed, and — separately — what was *committed*. The distinction
matters: advice received is not a commitment made, and conflating them creates
obligations nobody agreed to.

**Carries forward:** the commitment register.

### 7. Information requests during review

**Skill:** `map-agency-question-evidence`
Each question mapped to the evidence that answers it, and to the documents that must
change.

### 8. Post-marketing requirements and commitments

**Skills:** `negotiate-pmr-pmc-wording` *(planned, refuse-boundary)* ·
`execute-and-close-pmr-pmc` *(planned)*
Wording, design and timetable prepared; the negotiation refused.

### 9. Keep the dose rationale current

**Skill:** `maintain-living-dose-rationale` *(planned)*
Every commitment that changes the evidence base updates the rationale. This is where
post-approval evidence either stays connected to the original decision or drifts away
from it.

---

## What this workflow refuses

Representing the sponsor · defending a position live · negotiating wording · agreeing a
commitment · submitting anything. All five are the same refusal in different costumes:
**a person is accountable for what is said to a regulator.**

## The distinction worth holding onto

**Advice received ≠ commitment made.** Minutes conflate them constantly, and a
commitment register built by re-reading minutes six months later reliably invents
obligations that were never agreed. Separate them while the meeting is fresh.

## Contexts that change this workflow most

`rare-disease` — meetings often decide whether model-based evidence substitutes for a
study, which makes the applicant's position the central artifact · `oncology` — dose
optimisation expectations are frequently the subject, and the position must survive
*why not lower?*
