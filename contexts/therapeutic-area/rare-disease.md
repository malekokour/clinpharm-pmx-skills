# Context — rare disease and small-n development

**Dimension:** therapeutic area · **Attaches after selection; never selected**

Everything here follows from one constraint: **the sample size that conventional methods
assume does not exist and never will.** The response is not to lower the evidence bar
but to change which methods carry the weight.

## What small n breaks

| Method | Why it fails here | What carries the weight instead |
|---|---|---|
| Dedicated organ-impairment study | Not enough patients to enrol one | Physiologically-based modelling with a stated confidence basis |
| Dedicated interaction study | Same | In-vitro plus model-based prediction |
| Covariate identification by data | Underpowered; effects found are unstable | Mechanistic priors, informative priors, external data |
| Conventional dose-ranging | Cannot randomise across several levels | Modelling and simulation, target-engagement evidence |
| Empirical exposure–response | Too few events | Biomarker-anchored relationships |

Model-informed evidence is not a shortcut in this area — it is frequently the only
available route, and regulators have said so. That raises rather than lowers the
standard for how the model is qualified.

## Sampling and analysis

- **Sparse sampling** is the norm; population modelling is not optional.
- **Paediatric-heavy populations** are common, so growth and maturation are often
  in play at the same time as the disease.
- **Extrapolation across age or from an adjacent population** frequently carries the
  submission, and its assumptions must be explicit.
- **Every patient is informative.** Exclusion rules that would be routine elsewhere
  materially change the answer here, so state and justify them.

## Natural-history and external control

Where randomisation is impossible, comparison rests on natural history or an external
control. That places weight on data the sponsor may not have generated, and the
clinical-pharmacology contribution is often to characterise how comparable the
populations are on exposure-relevant covariates.

## Heterogeneity

Rare diseases are frequently genotypically heterogeneous under one clinical label.
Pooling across genotypes can hide both effect and variability. Whether the population is
one population is a question to answer, not assume.

## Regulatory posture

Accelerated and conditional pathways, biomarker-based endpoints, and post-approval
commitments are common. That makes the **living dose rationale** more important than
elsewhere: the evidence base is expected to change after approval, and the record of
what the dose rested on has to survive that change.

## What this context does not do

It does not lower the standard of evidence, accept an extrapolation, or decide whether
a model is qualified for a purpose. It states which conventional studies will not exist,
so a skill reports a substituted method rather than a gap.
