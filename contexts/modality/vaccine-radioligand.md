# Context — vaccine and radioligand therapy

**Dimension:** modality · **Attaches after selection; never selected**

Two modalities in one file because they share the property that matters most for
clinical pharmacology: **the conventional exposure metric is not the thing that governs
effect.** They differ in everything else, so the sections below are separate.

---

## Vaccines

### Exposure is immune response, not concentration

Systemic concentration of the antigen is usually irrelevant and often unmeasurable.
What stands in for exposure:

- Antibody titre and its kinetics — peak, decay, durability
- Seroconversion and seroprotection rates against a defined threshold
- Cell-mediated response where relevant
- Functional assays such as neutralisation, which may diverge from binding titre

**Dose–response exists but is shallow and plateaus**, so conventional dose-proportionality
reasoning misleads. Adjuvant and schedule frequently matter more than antigen amount,
which makes schedule a dose-finding variable in its own right.

### What replaces the standard framework

Organ-impairment and DDI studies are usually not the questions. The real interaction
questions are immunological: co-administration with another vaccine, and concomitant
immunosuppression that blunts response. Age is the dominant covariate — both extremes
respond differently, and that drives separate paediatric and older-adult evidence rather
than extrapolation.

### Durability and boosting

The clinical-pharmacology question is often *when does protection wane*, which is a
kinetics question about titre decay. Correlate-of-protection thresholds, where they
exist, are what make a titre interpretable; where none exists, say so rather than
implying one.

## Radioligand therapy

### Exposure is absorbed radiation dose

Plasma concentration is a poor proxy. The governing quantity is **absorbed dose in gray
to tumour and to organs at risk**, derived from imaging-based dosimetry over time.

- Tumour-to-organ dose ratio is the therapeutic index, and it is patient-specific
- Kidneys, bone marrow and salivary glands are the recurring organs at risk
- The dose-limiting constraint is usually cumulative absorbed dose to a normal organ,
  not a plasma exposure

### Two clocks run at once

Physical decay of the isotope and biological clearance of the ligand combine into an
effective half-life. Reasoning from either alone gives the wrong answer, and the
distinction has to be explicit in any exposure statement.

### What this changes downstream

- **Dosimetry substitutes for conventional dose-finding.** Administered activity in
  becquerels is the dose unit; the meaningful quantity is what it deposits.
- **Renal function matters** because the kidney is both a clearance route and a
  dose-limiting organ.
- **Handling, waste, and contact precautions** are part of the product's use, not an
  operational footnote.
- Prior therapy affecting marrow reserve constrains what is deliverable.

---

## What this context does not do

For neither modality does it judge whether an immune response is protective, whether a
dosimetry result supports an activity, or what the organ-at-risk limit should be. It
prevents a skill from computing a plasma AUC and presenting it as the exposure that
matters.
