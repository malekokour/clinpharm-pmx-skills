# Question-based review bank

The public anatomy of clinical pharmacology regulatory review, used to stress-test
a position **before** an agency does. Anchor: `mapp-4000-4` (Rev 1, September 2016).

> These are the shapes of questions the public review record shows being asked.
> They are not a prediction of what any agency will ask about a specific product.

## General clinical pharmacology

- What is the mechanism of action and how does the proposed indication follow from it?
- What is the basis for the proposed dose, regimen and route?
- What are the PK characteristics of the drug and its major active metabolites?
- Is exposure dose-proportional across the studied range? If not, what explains it?
- What is the accumulation on repeat dosing, and is it consistent with half-life?

## Exposure–response

- What exposure metric drives efficacy? What drives the principal safety events?
- Was the E-R relationship characterised for both, and are the conclusions consistent?
- Does the proposed dose sit where the E-R analysis supports it?
- What is the basis for dose modification thresholds?

## Intrinsic factors

- Renal impairment: which stages studied, what dose recommendation follows?
- Hepatic impairment: which classification, which categories studied?
- Age, weight, sex, race, pharmacogenomics — covered or justified as not required?
- Paediatric: extrapolation strategy and its assumptions?

## Extrinsic factors

- What is the DDI risk as perpetrator and as victim?
- Were clinical studies triggered by the in vitro results, and if waived, on what basis?
- Food effect: characterised at the clinically recommended dose?
- Acid-reducing agents: pH-dependent solubility assessed?

## Dataset and analysis

- Are the values in the summary traceable to the study reports?
- Do the label statements follow from the submitted data?
- Are the analysis populations and exclusions consistent across documents?

## How a skill uses this

Map each drafted position to the questions it must survive; flag positions with no
supporting evidence citation; report coverage as a fraction, never as "complete".
