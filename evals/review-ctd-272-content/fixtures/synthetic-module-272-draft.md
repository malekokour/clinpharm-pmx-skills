## 2.7.2.1 Background and Overview of Clinical Pharmacology

Six clinical pharmacology studies were conducted to characterise the
pharmacokinetics, the effect of intrinsic and extrinsic factors, and the
exposure–response relationship of relotinib.

The clinical pharmacology development approach was to establish single- and
multiple-dose pharmacokinetics in healthy adults, then to characterise the
effects of food, of a strong CYP3A inhibitor, and of renal and hepatic
impairment, and finally to integrate these in a population pharmacokinetic and
exposure–response analysis supporting the dose carried into Phase 3.

The recommended Phase 3 dose is 80 mg once daily, taken with food.

Concentrations of relotinib were measured using a validated LC-MS/MS method.
Method performance is summarised in section 2.7.2.5.

## 2.7.2.2 Summary of Results of Individual Studies

### RLT-1001 — Single and multiple ascending dose, healthy adults

Single doses of 10 to 160 mg were administered. In the multiple-dose part,
subjects received 40 mg twice daily for 14 days.

At steady state on Day 14, mean AUC0–24 was 1240.5 ng·h/mL and mean Cmax was
318 µg/mL. Median Tmax was 3.0 h and mean terminal half-life was 16.2 h.

### RLT-1004 — Drug interaction with azolimide, a strong CYP3A inhibitor

Relotinib 40 mg was administered alone and with azolimide at steady state.

### RLT-1007 — Renal impairment

Relotinib 40 mg single dose was administered to subjects with normal renal
function and to subjects with moderate and severe renal impairment. Exposure
increased with decreasing renal function.

### RLT-1009 — Cardiac repolarisation

A dedicated study assessed the effect of relotinib on cardiac repolarisation at
therapeutic and supratherapeutic exposures.

### RLT-1011 — Absolute bioavailability

Absolute bioavailability was 42% following a single oral dose with an
intravenous microtracer.

### RLT-1012 — Hepatic impairment

Relotinib 40 mg single dose was administered to subjects with mild and moderate
hepatic impairment and to matched controls.

### Population pharmacokinetic covariate analysis across studies

The population pharmacokinetic model developed in RLT-1010 identified body weight
and creatinine clearance as covariates on apparent clearance. Neither effect
exceeded the pre-specified 20% change in exposure across the observed covariate
range. The covariate analysis pooled data from RLT-1001, RLT-1004, RLT-1007 and
the Phase 2 study RLT-2001.

## 2.7.2.3 Comparison and Analyses of Results Across Studies

Exposure increased approximately dose-proportionally across the studied range.

Co-administration with azolimide decreased relotinib exposure by approximately
3.1-fold, and a dose-reduction instruction is proposed accordingly.

The exposure–response analysis demonstrated no clinically relevant relationship
between exposure and QTc interval.

Renal impairment increased exposure by 1.6-fold in the moderate group and
2.2-fold in the severe group. Supporting simulations are presented in Appendix
2.7.2.5.3.
